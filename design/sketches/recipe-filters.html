Find the right recipe, not just the one whose name you remember

Today the library is a grid and a name search. This sketch gives every recipe a few fixed tags (meal, kind, diet, free from), a heart, and the time it already has, then lets the library filter and sort by them. The tags mostly fill themselves in: recipe sites already say "vegan" and "soup" in the data our importer reads, and the AI import can pick from the same list.

The first three phones share one sample library of 14 recipes. Tap the chips, open the filter sheet with Filter, and press Show.

1 · The library

Chips under the search for the three filters you reach for most, one Filter chip for the rest. Everything lives in the URL like the search does today (?q=&meal=dinner&diet=vegetarian&max=30), so a filtered list survives a reload, the back button and a link sent to your partner.

2 · Where the tags come from

Filters are only as good as the tags, and nobody wants to tag 40 recipes by hand. So: the importer fills them from the page, the AI picks from the same fixed list, the form shows them as a suggestion to check, and existing recipes get a short sorting pass, one card at a time.

3 · Planning a meal

The plan sheet [3d] already asks "Which meal?" first. With tags, that answer can order the list: breakfast recipes first when you plan a breakfast. It orders rather than hides, because Saturday's lunch is often last night's soup.

The vocabulary

A fixed list, not free tags: fixed words can be translated, stay short enough for one chip row, and give the importer and the AI something to map onto. Household content stays untranslated; these are the app's words, like the meal slots.

  • MealBreakfast · Lunch · Dinner · SnackSeveral per recipe. Exactly the plan's four slots, so the plan sheet can use them.Frühstück · Mittagessen · Abendessen · Snack
  • KindMain · Soup · Salad · Side · Dessert · BakingSeveral per recipe (strudel is dessert and baking). "Type of meal" in the everyday sense.Hauptspeise · Suppe · Salat · Beilage · Nachspeise · Backen
  • DietMeat · Fish · Vegetarian · VeganOne per recipe, or none yet. A ladder: filtering vegetarian also shows vegan.Fleisch · Fisch · Vegetarisch · Vegan
  • Free fromGluten · LactoseSeparate flags, all must hold. Only two, the ones that come up at a table.Glutenfrei · Laktosefrei
  • TimeAny · 20 · 30 · 45 · 60 minFrom the time field recipes already have. A recipe with no time drops out once a cap is set.Egal · bis 20 · 30 · 45 · 60 Min.
  • Favourite♥ on the recipeOne heart per household (see the questions below).Favoriten
  • SortNewest · A–Z · Quickest · Not cooked latelyThe last one reads the meal plan: when was each recipe last on a day. Never planned sorts first.Neueste · A–Z · Schnellste · Länger nicht gekocht

How it's wired

PieceWhat changes
DataA recipe_tags table (recipe_id, tag) with keys from a fixed list in lib/utils/recipe-tags.ts (meal:dinner, kind:soup, free:gluten), cascading with the recipe. recipes gains diet (enum, NULL = not set) and favourite. One migration.
URL & loadThe library's GET form grows hidden fields: q, meal, kind, diet, free (repeatable), max, fav, sort. The load parses them into one RecipeFilter, unknown values ignored, and listRecipes builds the where. Chips are links/submits, so it works without JavaScript too.
Live countThe load also returns a small index (id, tags, diet, minutes, favourite) for the whole library. The sheet counts against it in the browser; the grid itself still comes from the server.
Searchq matches recipe names or ingredient names (same name_key matching the shopping list uses).
Sort "not cooked lately"max(meals.date) per recipe_id, a left join, NULL first.
Link importrecipe-import.ts starts reading recipeCategory, recipeCuisine, suitableForDiet and keywords, mapped through an English + German word list ("Suppe", "Hauptgericht", "Frühstück", "vegan"). Matches arrive as suggested chips; no match, no tag.
AI importThe extraction schema gets the same fixed keys as an enum, so the model can only pick from the list.
Diet guessFor the sorting pass only: no ingredient matches a meat or fish word list → suggest vegetarian; no dairy/egg/honey word either → suggest vegan. Always shown as a guess, never saved without a tap.
Plan sheetOrders its list by the chosen slot's meal: tag, plus the three quick chips. Client-side, like its search.
CopyTag names in en.ts/de.ts under cooking.tags; the keys never show.

Questions for you

  • Fixed list or your own tags?The sketch uses a fixed list (translatable, maps from imports). Own tags ("Oma's", "Party") could come later as one extra group.
  • Is the list right?Anything missing for your kitchen? Cuisine (Austrian, Italian, Asian…) is the obvious candidate; I left it out to keep the sheet short.
  • One heart or one per person?The sketch has one per household. Per person would let the filter say "Lukas's favourites", at the cost of a second row of faces.
  • Which three quick chips?Sketch: Favourites · Under 30 min · Vegetarian. Could instead follow the clock (Breakfast in the morning, Dinner after 3 pm).
  • Build orderSuggest two steps: 1) tags + form + filters + sheet, 2) importer/AI mapping, sorting pass and plan-sheet ordering.
