Home Decided

A gear above the raccoon

The corner over the raccoon's ears is the one free spot in Home's header. The button is the same round one Expenses uses for its menu.

What changes on Home

  • A round gear button, 38px, in the top-right corner. A screen reader calls it "Settings".
  • The faces stay, 28px, straight on the page: no pill, no chevron, no link. They show who lives here and nothing more.
  • The header's shortest height grows from 170 to 182px. Only a two-line greeting notices: its cards start 12px lower.
  • No other tab changes.

What was checked

  • All 71 Home raccoons were measured under the gear's corner. In 36 of them an ear or a hat comes within 10px of the top of the picture there, and in 7 it touches it. So the gap can't be borrowed from empty space in the art.
  • With 182px there are at least 10px between button and raccoon in every pose, in both languages. The numbers under the phones are measured from these drawings.
  • Still to check once it is built: 200% text size, and a real phone with a notch.

Your question

Long names beside the raccoon

This already works today, and the gear doesn't change it. The greeting wraps in the column the raccoon leaves, and the header grows downwards.

How it works today

  • The greeting gets the width the raccoon leaves: the screen minus the raccoon's reach, and never less than 150px kept free. On a 390px phone that is a column of 177 to 196px, depending on the pose.
  • A display name can be 40 characters. It breaks at spaces and hyphens first, and inside a word only when one word is wider than the column.
  • Every extra line makes the header taller. The raccoon sits on the first card's edge, so it moves down with the card and text never runs under it.
  • Nothing is cut off and the type doesn't shrink. The household name on top (up to 60 characters) wraps in the same column.
  • The one rough edge is the third phone: a single word of about twelve letters or more can break without a hyphen. Turning on automatic hyphenation for the greeting would give "Bartholo-mäus" instead. Small, and separate from the gear.

With the gear

  • The gear sits right of that column and above the raccoon, so no name can reach it.
  • A longer name pushes the raccoon further down, away from the gear. The short greeting is the tight case, and that is the one the 12px are for.

Legal Agreed as sketched

Impressum and Datenschutz

Two public pages with two entrances each: the sign-in screen, because that is the first place the app asks for an email address, and Settings → About. From Home they are three taps away.

How it gets built

  • Two routes outside the household guard, for example /legal/imprint and /legal/privacy.
  • The text is the operator's, not the app's. It comes from the deploy (a file or a variable), and the links hide while it is missing, the way email sign-in hides without mail settings. Otherwise someone else's install would show your address.
  • German is the version that counts. English can follow.

What the privacy text has to name

  • Sign-in: Google, or a one-time code sent through your mail provider.
  • Hosting: your server, the database, uploaded recipe photos.
  • Backups: the database is copied to Backblaze B2.
  • Push: notifications travel through the browser maker's push service.
  • AI features: recipe pages and photos go to the provider the household picked, with its own key.
  • Feedback: "Send feedback" is filed as a GitHub issue.
  • Cookies: session, language, appearance, invite code. All of them are needed to run the app.

This list is read from the code, and it is not legal advice. For a private, non-commercial site the Austrian disclosure is usually short, but both texts should be checked once by someone who does this for a living.
