Keep to the schedule

Today a recurring task's next date counts from the day it was ticked off, so it drifts every time someone is late. With one new switch in the task sheet, a task can keep to the calendar instead, for example "monthly, on the first Sunday".

The phones work. Set up the task in the first one, pick a tick-off day under the second one, and watch the list in the third.

The rules behind it

How the next date is worked out when the switch is on.

CaseWhat happens
Done on time or lateThe next date is the next one on the schedule after the date it was due. Doing it late doesn't move the schedule.
Done very late (past the next date too)It skips to the next date that's still ahead. Missed ones don't pile up as extra tasks.
Done earlyThe same: the next date after the one it was due. Ticking off "Deep clean" on Saturday for Sunday's date doesn't make it due again tomorrow.
Monthly, "on the 31st"Falls on the last day of shorter months (30 Nov, 28 Feb), like recurring expenses. A 29th–31st reads "on the 31st (or the last day)".
First due in the month's last 7 daysThe weekday choice becomes "the last Sunday" instead of "the fourth/fifth Sunday", so it never skips a month.
Skip this timeMoves on to the next date on the schedule, same as done.
Snooze / rescheduleMoves only this one time. The schedule stays, and the time after is back on track.
Away (holiday pause)A task due while you're away still moves to after your return (#165). After that one, it's back on the schedule.
Every 2 weeks / every 3 monthsWorks the same way: "every 2 months, on the first Sunday".

For you to decide

  • The name of the switch"Keep to the schedule" in the sketch. Other options: "Fixed dates", "Same day each time". German maybe "Fester Termin" or "Im Rhythmus bleiben".
  • Default for new tasksOff in the sketch (counts from the last time, as today). Should monthly tasks start with it on?
  • Weekly too?For weekly tasks it keeps the weekday: done Tuesday instead of Sunday, still due next Sunday. Worth it, or monthly only?
